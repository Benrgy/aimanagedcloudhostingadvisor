Why I switched

For years I was paying $220/month for managed WordPress hosting. The promise was simple: pay more, get better performance and support. The reality? My site averaged 2.1 seconds load time, my PageSpeed score hovered around 61, and whenever something broke, I submitted a ticket and waited 6–12 hours for a response.

I was essentially paying premium prices for shared-hosting-tier performance. The “managed” part meant they handled updates — that was about it. When a friend mentioned Cloudways, I was skeptical. Another hosting company making big promises. But the price point — starting at $12/month — made it low-risk to try.

The migration

I expected migrating to be painful. I was wrong. Cloudways has a built-in WordPress migrator plugin that handled my main site in about 25 minutes. I tested everything on a staging domain before pointing my DNS, which was smart — there was one plugin conflict I caught and fixed before going live.

The total downtime when I switched DNS? Under 3 minutes. In 2+ years since, I've had zero unplanned downtime that lasted more than a few seconds.

What surprised me

Three things immediately stood out:

  • Breaker cache is legitimately excellent. I'd used WP Super Cache, W3 Total Cache, and WP Rocket. Breaker (Cloudways' built-in caching layer) outperformed all of them with zero configuration on my part.
  • The server panel is developer-friendly without being overwhelming. I'm technical but not a sysadmin. The Cloudways dashboard gives me PHP version control, Redis toggle, SSH access, and staging environments — all in a clean UI.
  • Live chat support actually resolves things. I've chatted with support 11 times in 2 years. Average resolution time in my experience: 8 minutes. That's better than most “premium” hosts I've paid 3x as much for.

The numbers after 2 years

Let me be specific, because vague performance claims are useless:

  • Average load time (GTmetrix): 0.8 seconds (was 2.1s)
  • TTFB: 0.19 seconds (was 1.4s)
  • Google PageSpeed Mobile: 91 (was 58)
  • Google PageSpeed Desktop: 97 (was 65)
  • Uptime over 24 months: 99.94%
  • Monthly cost: $39 (was $220)

Who should switch to Cloudways

Cloudways is ideal if you: run one or more WordPress/WooCommerce sites, are currently on shared hosting or an overpriced managed host, have some technical comfort (you're not afraid of a server dashboard), and care about performance and uptime over hand-holding.

Who should NOT use Cloudways

Be honest with yourself: if you need email hosting (Cloudways doesn't provide it), want zero technical involvement (look at Kinsta or WP Engine instead), or are running a simple blog with under 1,000 monthly visitors (shared hosting is fine), Cloudways may be overkill.
