What to Avoid: Red Flags in Managed Hosting

After 2+ years in this space, I have learned the warning signs of bad managed hosting:

⚠
$1-3/mo "managed" hosting
Real managed hosting cannot be profitable at $3/month. These are shared hosting plans rebranded with the word "managed." You get shared resources with none of the actual management.
⚠
Unlimited everything at low prices
"Unlimited bandwidth," "unlimited sites," "unlimited storage" — these claims are only possible on shared infrastructure where limits are hidden in the terms of service. Read the fair use policy.
⚠
No clear server specifications
If a host does not tell you how much RAM, CPU, and storage you get, you are almost certainly on shared infrastructure. Real cloud hosting is transparent about resources.
⚠
Introductory prices without clear renewal rates
Some hosts advertise $2.99/mo but charge $25/mo on renewal. Always check the renewal price before committing. Cloudways has no introductory pricing — the listed price is permanent.
⚠
No uptime guarantee or vague SLA
Reputable managed hosts guarantee 99.9% uptime or better and have clear compensation policies for downtime. Avoid hosts that are vague about their uptime commitments.

What is the difference between managed hosting and cloud hosting?

Cloud hosting refers to the infrastructure: your site runs on scalable cloud servers (DigitalOcean, AWS, etc.) rather than a single physical server. Managed hosting refers to the service layer: the provider handles server maintenance, security updates, and performance optimization. Managed cloud hosting combines both — cloud infrastructure with a managed service layer on top. Cloudways is the best example of this category.
